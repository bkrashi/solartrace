An investigation dashboard for solar supply chains

Clean energy should not depend on forced labour.

A solar panel passes through about six factories before it reaches a rooftop. Much of the world's polysilicon, the purified silicon inside every cell, comes from a region where government-imposed forced labour has been documented. SolarTrace follows a panel back through each step and tells you how much of the trail is proven, how much is suspected, and where the records run out.

Six factories, five borders, one question

Forced-labour risk is usually concentrated at the start of the chain, in silicon smelting and polysilicon. By the time a panel is assembled in Southeast Asia, its origin is hard to see.

    How to read this dashboard

    Every claim carries its evidence label

    Investigations fail when a hunch is presented as a fact, or when a missing record is treated as proof that nothing is wrong. SolarTrace keeps three categories apart.

    Verified record

    A document we can point to, such as a registry filing, a bill of lading or a customs declaration. Drawn as a solid green line.

    Suspected exposure

    Indicators point to a link, such as a press announcement, a shared director or a shared location, but no direct record confirms it. Drawn as a dashed amber line.

    Missing information

    We looked and could not find the record needed to answer the question. This is not an accusation, but it is not a clean result either. Drawn as a dotted grey line.

    Where the evidence comes from

    Three sources, three different jobs

    Sayari

    Corporate registries, ownership chains, officers and shipment records from 250+ jurisdictions. Answers the question: who owns whom?

    Tradeverifyd

    Trade relationships, risk annotations and entity scores. Answers the question: who ships to whom, and what has been reported about them?

    Tavily

    Search over public reporting from government notices, NGO research and journalism. Answers the question: what is already publicly known?

    What we know, what we suspect, what's missing

    Each finding separates its evidence into the three categories. Record links to Sayari and Tradeverifyd are sample placeholders. Links marked Tavily open real public documents.

    From sample to live

    Connect the Sayari and Tradeverifyd MCP servers and SolarTrace replaces the sample records with real entity profiles, shipment sets and annotations. The evidence labels follow the same rules: if it has a record, it is verified; if it rests on indicators, it is suspected; if we can't find it, we say so.
